Cost questions answered

Indicative figures place most full kitchen renovations between $25,000 and $60,000 fully fitted. A budget refresh covering door fronts, benchtop and splashback without moving the layout typically lands between $12,000 and $30,000. Premium renovations with natural stone, custom joinery and a butler's pantry can exceed $80,000. Treat these as screening tools rather than quotes.

  • Why quotes differ: benchtop material, joinery detail, appliance allowances, splashback specification and trade changes all move the result.
  • What to compare first: if two quotes sit far apart, compare exclusions before assuming one renovator is cheaper.
  • When the price firms up: the in-home consult turns measurements and finish selections into a fixed price.

Use the early range to test whether the desired scope is realistic, then rely on the written fixed-price quote after measurement for the actual figure.

Timeline and process questions

For a mid-range full renovation, allow roughly 6 to 12 weeks from contract signing to handover. The sequence breaks into about 4 to 6 weeks for cabinetry and stone fabrication, 2 to 4 weeks of on-site work once cabinetry is ready, and 1 to 2 weeks for finishing.

The visible demolition and install stage is only part of the job. The front half is driven by cabinet, stone and appliance lead times, so the practical timeline question is not simply when demolition starts, but when design sign-off, site measurements, ordering and fabrication are locked.

Before signing, ask the renovator to identify the decisions that can delay fabrication: final layout, benchtop choice, hardware selection and appliance dimensions.

Who this applies to

This guidance applies to Melbourne homeowners comparing a budget refresh, a mid-range full renovation, a premium kitchen with custom joinery, or a compact apartment kitchen. It also suits briefs that include period-home detailing, post-war brick homes, or a larger layout that may include a butler's pantry.

Period terraces, Edwardians and bungalows may suit made-to-measure joinery rather than a flat-pack run, especially where the aim is to respect existing character while improving storage and function. Apartment kitchens across Richmond, Brunswick, Footscray and the inner city need a different brief, with owners corporation documentation, compact layouts, acoustic underlay and clever storage all in play.

It is less useful for readers who only need appliance replacement, painting or a single trade repair, because a full renovation moves through design, quotation, contract and build. For those smaller jobs, the right question is whether the work needs a full renovator or a narrower trade quote.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • Does the quote include design, manufacture, demolition, installation and finishing?
  • Are plumbing or electrical changes included, or treated as variations?
  • Are the hardware brand and soft-close runners included in the base price?
  • For a larger layout, is a butler's pantry being modelled or simply assumed?

Where a project is large enough to require a formal domestic building pathway, ask how the written contract, transparent variation process and any required domestic building insurance will be handled. General renovation budgeting principles from ASIC Moneysmart also support comparing quotes in writing before committing.

  1. Define the scope. Write down whether the job is a refresh, full renovation, custom joinery project or apartment kitchen before asking for a quote.
  2. Set a rough budget. Use the indicative ranges to decide whether the desired finish level is realistic before the in-home consult.
  3. Prepare property details. Have the suburb, property type, kitchen size and any owners corporation requirements ready for the renovator callback.
  4. Check the written quote. Confirm inclusions, exclusions, hardware, trades, fabrication lead time and the variation process before signing.

Common questions

What is the difference between a refresh and a full renovation? A refresh updates door fronts, benchtop and splashback without moving the layout. A full renovation can include new cabinetry, benchtops, plumbing and electrical updates, splashback and soft-close hardware, with design, manufacture, demolition and installation handled under a fixed-price written contract.

How accurate are published cost ranges? They are indicative screening figures, not substitutes for a written fixed-price quote after measurement. If two quotes are far apart, compare exclusions such as benchtop material, joinery detail, appliance allowances and splashback specification before assuming one renovator is cheaper.

What should I prepare before the in-home consultation? Define the scope, note the suburb, property type and kitchen size, set a rough budget from the indicative ranges, and record any owners corporation requirements so the renovator can review project size, style and timing.
